# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mid-market B2B SaaS and DTC founders, and marketing directors with revenue pressure. They are sophisticated buyers who understand funnels, paid ads, automation, and attribution. They evaluate agencies skeptically — they've been burned by generic retainers before. They want proof of results before a conversation, not a sales deck. They use this site while researching vendors, often on desktop, in a deliberate decision-making context.

## Product Purpose

Miduva is a custom growth systems agency. It designs and builds tailored growth systems using paid ads, funnels, automation, and data — engineered end-to-end, owned by the client. The landing page exists to convert high-intent leads who are ready to build real infrastructure. Success is a qualified intro call booked through the contact form.

## Brand Personality

**Direct. Sophisticated. Confident.**

Miduva cuts through agency fluff without becoming cold. The voice says what it does, backs every claim with real numbers, and trusts the reader to be smart enough to appreciate that. It's a founder talking to another founder — not a salesperson closing, not a consultant hedging. Warm enough to be human, confident enough to never oversell.

Three-word personality: **Earned. Precise. Human.**

The tone is anti-hype by design. "The machine that fixes all three traps" is the register — engineering language used naturally, not performatively.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS cream**: Light backgrounds, pastel accents, rounded cards, emoji checkmarks — the HubSpot/Notion/Webflow template look. Miduva is not a software product; its design should not look like one.
- **Hype-agency flash**: Neon gradients, stock-photo team shots, giant fake percentages, hero testimonials in yellow callouts — the WebFX / Neil Patel / DigitalMarketer aesthetic. Miduva earns credibility through specificity, not volume.
- **Corp enterprise**: Navy suits, stock photography, lifeless B2B blue — the IBM-ified, committee-approved aesthetic. Miduva is founder-led and should feel like it.
- **Over-designed brutalism**: Extreme type scales, intentional ugliness, legibility sacrificed for edge — Miduva's sophistication is in precision, not provocation.

## Design Principles

1. **Proof over promise.** Claims earn their space by being specific. "4.8× ROAS" not "exceptional results". If it can't be substantiated, it shouldn't be said.
2. **The machine metaphor.** Miduva builds systems, not campaigns. Design should feel engineered — intentional, structured, load-bearing — not decorated.
3. **Confidence needs no volume.** Dark, quiet backgrounds let content speak. Don't shout. A restrained layout with one precise accent reads more authoritative than a cluttered one.
4. **Warm but never soft.** The system is technical; the voice is human. Motion, copy, and interaction design should feel like a thoughtful person made deliberate choices — not like a template ran.
5. **Earned asymmetry.** Grid breaks, oversized type, and spatial contrast are tools of emphasis — used where there is something worth emphasizing, not by default.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target: WCAG 2.1 AA.

- All text/background combinations must meet 4.5:1 contrast ratio (normal text) and 3:1 (large text/UI components).
- All interactive elements must be keyboard-navigable with visible focus states.
- Animations must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Scroll-triggered and entrance animations should collapse to instant/opacity-only under that preference.
- Dark-first theme is the default; light mode is available. Both must independently pass AA contrast.
- No color as the sole conveyor of information (form errors, states, etc. need text or icon support).
